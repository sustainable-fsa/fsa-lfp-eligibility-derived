Codified in
[Title 43 U.S. Code Chapter 46](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title43/chapter46&edition=prelim), the
Geospatial Data Act (GDA) establishes clear responsibilities for federal agencies:

[§ 2805 – National Geospatial Data Asset Data Themes](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title43-section2805&num=0&edition=prelim):

-   Establishes National Geospatial Data Assets (NGDA) data themes as standardized categories of geospatial data used
    across federal agencies.
-   Designates lead covered agencies responsible for managing each theme's datasets, resources, and services.
-   Requires lead agencies to develop standards, coordinate data population efforts, and ensure data quality and
    accessibility.
-   Mandates annual reporting on the status and progress of each NGDA theme.

[§ 2808 – Covered Agency Responsibilities](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title43-section2808&num=0&edition=prelim):

-   Agencies must use geospatial data standards, including metadata standards, and make metadata available through the
    [GeoPlatform](https://geoplatform.gov).
-   Agencies must search existing sources, including the GeoPlatform, before spending federal funds on new data
    collection.
-   Agencies must report annually on their geospatial data activities and compliance. The
    [2024 USDA Covered Agency Report](https://www.fgdc.gov/gda/gda-ca-reports/fy2024/fy2024-usda-gda-covered-agency-report.pdf)
    is available on the FGDC website. Notable are the USDA's responses to question 12.1 and 12.2 (pp. 20–21), which
    acknowledges that the USDA recieves geospatial data from non-Federal sources and asserts that the USDA employs
    methods to ensure quality in geospatial data collected from non-federal sources.

[§ 2809 – Limitation on Use of Federal Funds](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title43-section2809&num=0&edition=prelim):

-   Federal funds may not be used to collect, produce, acquire, maintain, or disseminate geospatial data unless the data
    complies with FGDC standards.

In response to the GDA, the Office of Management and Budget released
[OMB Circular A–16](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Circular-016.pdf), a binding federal policy
document that:

-   Directs agencies to coordinate geospatial data activities.
-   Establishes the Federal Geographic Data Committee (FGDC).
-   Defines the National Spatial Data Infrastructure (NSDI) and NGDA themes.

The "Governmental Units and Administrative and Statistical Boundaries" theme is one of the 18 NGDA themes established by
OMB Circular A–16 (Appendix E) under the under Section 2805(b)(3)(A) of the Geospatial Data Act.

> **Governmental Units**: These data describe, by a consistent set of rules and semantic definitions, the official
> boundary of federal, state, local, and tribal governments as reported/certified to the U.S. Census Bureau by
> responsible officials of each government for purposes of reporting the Nation's official statistics.

It includes core datasets such as:

-   County and state boundaries
-   Tribal lands
-   Congressional districts
-   School districts
-   Census tracts and blocks

The U.S. Department of Commerce is the Lead Covered Agency (LCA), and the U.S. Census Bureau is the Theme Lead. The
current County and Equivalent Entities National TIGER/Line Shapefiles are among the Governmental Units NGDAs listed in
the
[Governmental Units, and Administrative and Statistical Boundaries Theme data portal](https://ngda-gov-units-geoplatform.hub.arcgis.com).
To maintain and improve the Governmental Units NGDAs, the Census Bureau operates the
[Boundary and Annexation Survey (BAS)](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/bas.html). The BAS website provides its
rationale and authorizing legislation (links added):

> The Boundary and Annexation Survey (BAS) provides eligible governments, which include tribal, state, and
> general-purpose local governments, an opportunity to review the Census Bureau’s legal boundary data to confirm the
> Census Bureau has the correct boundary, name, and status information. BAS also allows participants to review and
> provide updates to census designated places (CDPs) and linear features. Maintaining correct boundaries helps make sure
> that the Census Bureau assigns the appropriate housing and population counts to each government.
> [Title 13, Section 6, United States Code](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title13-section6&num=0&edition=prelim)
> authorizes this survey.
>
> The federal government allocates more than \$2.8 trillion in federal funds annually for health, welfare,
> infrastructure, education, and other federal programs and services. Maintaining correct boundaries helps make sure
> that the Census Bureau assigns the appropriate housing and population counts to each government so that governments
> receive funds appropriately and have the best data available for decision-making.
>
> BAS fulfills the agency’s responsibility as part of the National Spatial Data Infrastructure, for which the Office of
> Management and Budget (OMB) Circular A–16 designates the Census Bureau as the lead federal agency for maintaining
> national data about legal government boundaries, as well as administrative and statistical boundaries. BAS supports
> the geospatial data steward responsibilities of the Geospatial Data Act, the Evidence Act, OMB E-Gov, the Federal
> Geographic Data Committee, Data.gov, GeoPlatform.gov, the National Map, the Geographic Names Information System, and
> the Geospatial One-Stop.

Finally,
[February 2025 guidance from the National Geospatial Advisory Committee](https://www.fgdc.gov/ngac/meetings/feb-2025/ngac-paper-advancing-the-nations-geospatial.pdf)
recommended that priority national datasets, including NGDA themes, be treated as authoritative and shared in
ready-to-use formats. It urges FGDC to define "authoritative geospatial data" (p. 6) and monitor its use across
agencies.
